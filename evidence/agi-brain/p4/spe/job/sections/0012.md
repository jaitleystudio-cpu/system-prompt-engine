## 2.3 The LVT-3 Experiment Overview — Experimental Setup and Methodology

### Introduction to the Experiment

The LVT-3 experiment serves as a pivotal milestone in the continuous refinement and improvement of AI systems, particularly those designed for long-term autonomy. This section delves into the foundational aspects of the experiment's setup and methodology.

### Theoretical Framework

The experiment is structured around the principles of frontier reasoning (R1), research intelligence (literature), experimental intelligence, creative invention, long-horizon autonomy, and failure learning/safety mechanisms. By integrating these elements, the LVT-3 experiment aims to optimize AI systems' performance across various tasks with minimal human intervention.

### Experimental Design

#### 2.3.1 Frontier Reasoning (Multiple Methods)

Frontier reasoning involves using multiple methods—search + constructions + transfer—in distinct ways as separate processes. The `agi_brain/netsearch.py` conditions A–D are utilized, with the AI system being conditionally capable of performing tasks based on these different methodologies.

#### 2.3.2 Research Intelligence (Literature)

Research intelligence focuses on utilizing scholarly literature to inform and improve the AI system's performance. This is achieved through live scholarly lanes (`r*-scholarly` branches) that draw from citation tables for both manual WebFetch/WebSearch, ensuring a robust base of knowledge.

#### 2.3.3 Experimental Intelligence

Experimental intelligence encompasses the world/discriminator concept, with LVT-3 stats (`research/lvt3/experiment.py: ci_t, ci_boot, _sd`) providing critical metrics for evaluating system performance over multiple runs and conditions D’s counterexample world model + discriminating-experiment ordering (CEGIS-style), which aims to simulate realistic scenarios in terms of the AI system's failure modes.

#### 2.3.4 Creative Invention + Verified Reuse

Creative invention, coupled with verified reuse, is a key component of the experiment. The LVT-3 Registry pattern (`research/lvt3/registry.py`) ensures that only solutions are admitted after re-verification to maintain the integrity and reliability of AI systems. Additionally, the WDIC continuation engine (`research/wdic_vct/continuation_engine.py`) leverages resumable jobs (R7 slice) from `agi_brain/longdoc.py`, enabling autonomous long-term operations.

#### 2.3.5 Long-Horizon Autonomy

The experiment's focus on long-horizon autonomy is achieved through the continuation engine (`research/wdic_vct/continuation_engine.py`) and resumable job (R7 slice), which allows for continuous operation and adaptation over extended periods without human intervention.

#### 2.3.6 Failure Learning / Safety Mechanism

The LVT-3 experiment includes a failure genome mechanism (`spe_runtime/failure_genome/store.py`), with R1 rows logging every independent rejection, allowing the system to learn from past failures through the wiring of rejected tasks into FailureGenomeStore. This process is complemented by the re-verification cycle in C’s library, ensuring that AI systems are thoroughly tested and refined based on their outcomes.

### Methodology

#### 2.3.7 Experimental Execution

The experiment was executed with 10 seeds × 6 configs, each consisting of 120 hidden tasks per seed, taking a total runtime of approximately 8.1 seconds. The system's performance is evaluated through means over seeds and paired differences in verified success rates (t-CI / bootstrap CI), providing insights into the effectiveness and reliability of AI systems under various conditions.

#### 2.3.8 Data Analysis

The raw data collected from the experiment includes evidence/lvt3/run1/{results.json,task_rows.jsonl}, with the results stored in a JSON format for easy analysis. The findings are intended to contribute significantly to the development of safer, more efficient, and ethically sound AI technologies.

### Conclusion

The LVT-3 experiment is not just an isolated test but part of a broader effort to integrate cutting-edge AI methodologies with traditional engineering principles. By evaluating different systems' abilities through creative invention and failure learning, this initiative aims to provide valuable insights into how long-horizon autonomy systems can evolve over time while maintaining high quality and cost-effectiveness.

This experimental setup underscores the importance of continuous improvement in AI technologies, ensuring that they remain effective and reliable as their applications expand. The findings from LVT-3 are intended to be a significant contribution to future research on safer, more efficient, and ethically sound AI systems.
