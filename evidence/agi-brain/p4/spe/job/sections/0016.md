## 2.7 The LVT-3 Experiment Overview — Pre-Experiment Measures and Control Variables

**2.7 The LVT-3 Experiment Overview — Pre-Experiment Measures and Control Variables** Before delving into the specifics of the LVT-3 experiment, it is essential to understand the pre-experiment measures and control variables that were implemented to ensure a fair and controlled environment for the participants. ### 2.7.1 Preparation and Setup The LVT-3 experiment was meticulously planned with several key preparatory steps aimed at minimizing any potential biases or confounding variables. The first step involved defining clear objectives and scope, ensuring that all aspects of the experiment were thoroughly understood by both the builder and the independent verifier. **Preparation:** 1. **Objective Definition:** A brief owner’s brief text was provided to the builder detailing the primary goal of the experiment. This included specifying conditions such as: - The use of frozen experiments. - No training or retraining of model weights, learning refers only to admission and reuse of qualified procedures. - All tasks were deterministic synthetic integer-list transformation programs. 2. **Scope Clarification:** The scope note provided a detailed overview of the experiment’s domain program DSL (Domain Specific Language) and input requirements. This included specifying that: - Inputs could be generated by a visible generator, which was underspecified to allow for various test scenarios. - The goal was to evaluate the performance of different configurations in terms of quality, false success rates, and cost. 3. **Implementation Details:** - The experiment used 10 seeds (families) with each seed having 14 held-out test families. - Statistical units were defined as individual seeds within the test families. - Quality was evaluated based on a pruned merge-exchange size of 25,000 items in the frozen table `experiment_r1.REF`. - False success rates were tracked across independent evaluators. ### 2.7.2 Control Variables To ensure that all participants were treated equally and to avoid any unintended biases, several control variables were implemented during the pre-experiment phase: **Control Variable:** - **Visibility of Data:** The experiment was designed with a strict protocol regarding visibility of data. While the builder had access to the full details of the experiment's scope and objectives, they could not influence any aspects of the test families or the evaluation process. **Control Variable:** - **Model Weight Training:** All model weights used in the experiment were pre-trained and frozen at a specific point before the actual experiment began. This ensured that there was no learning during the run itself, thus eliminating potential biases introduced by retraining procedures. **Control Variable:** - **Input Generation:** The input generation process remained completely under control of the builder, ensuring consistency across all test scenarios. The visible generator provided inputs to the experiment but did not influence the outcome or the evaluation process in any way. ### 2.7.3 Experimental Design The LVT-3 experiment design was structured around three primary variables: configurations (A-D), false success rates, and quality metrics. Each variable was carefully controlled to ensure that their effects could be accurately measured and analyzed: **Experimental Design Variables:** 1. **Configurations A-D:** - Configuration A: The simplest configuration, involving only basic operations. - Configuration B: Added a more complex operation (mul(k) k∈{-3,-2,-1,1,2,3}). - Configuration C: Introduced another complex operation (neg), and D included the highest complexity operation (rev). 2. **False Success Rates:** - The independent evaluator measured the percentage of times participants claimed a network as valid that was actually invalid. 3. **Quality Metrics:** - Quality was evaluated based on the size of the pruned merge-exchange structure, which was a measure of how efficiently and effectively the participant’s solution could be reduced to its core components. - The quality metric for each configuration was calculated by comparing it with the best-known size in Knuth’s TAOCP 5.3.4 section. ### 2.7.4 Statistical Analysis To ensure that the results of the experiment were statistically robust and reliable, several statistical measures were employed: **Statistical Measures:** 1. **t-CI (Tukey–Kramer Interquartile Range):** - The Bonferroni-adjusted t-CI was used to determine if any differences between configurations or false success rates were significant. - This method adjusted for multiple comparisons, reducing the likelihood of a Type I error. 2.
