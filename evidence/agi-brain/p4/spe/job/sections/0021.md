## 3.2 The AGI-BRAIN R1 Study Details — Methodology of Data Collection and Analysis

### 3.2 The AGI-BRAIN R1 Study Details — Methodology of Data Collection and Analysis #### Introduction The AGI-BRAIN R1 study was a comprehensive research endeavor that aimed to evaluate the performance of comparator networks in discovering valid structures within complex data sets. This section delves into the methodology used to collect, process, and analyze the data collected during this study. #### 3.2.1 Data Collection Strategy **Objective**: The primary goal of collecting data was to assess the effectiveness of various search algorithms against a benchmark task: the discovery of valid comparator networks within constrained conditions. This included both plain searches and enhanced methods such as B-search with tools, C+ verified library, and D+ world model. **Data Sources**: - **Plain Search**: The study started from an insertion network and iteratively applied hill-climbing to optimize for quality without exhaustive tool validation on a fixed sample of 32 vectors. This ensured that the initial hypothesis was well-defined but did not rely heavily on external validation tools. - **B-search with Tools**: In this method, the Knuth merge-exchange construction served as the starting point, complemented by an exhaustively validating tool and redundancy-pruning before and after the search phase. The goal here was to explore how tools could enhance the efficiency of the search process while maintaining a thorough validation. - **C+ Verified Library**: This condition utilized a verified library that included both a world model and a detailed set of valid networks, serving as a reference for validating new candidate networks discovered through the search algorithms. - **D+ World Model**: The final configuration involved the use of a world model to enhance the depth of the comparison process. While not exhaustive in the traditional sense, it provided a richer context that could indirectly improve network quality. **Data Processing and Analysis Tools**: - **Paired t-tests**: To determine statistical significance between different conditions, paired t-tests were applied. These tests compared the means of results from two groups to identify significant differences. - **Bootstrapping**: The study also utilized bootstrapping methods for more robust estimation of performance metrics such as false acceptance rates (FAR), which is crucial in evaluating the reliability and generalizability of results. - **Regression Analysis**: Where relevant, regression models were employed to predict outcomes based on factors influencing the experiment. This included examining correlations between various conditions and their impact on network quality. #### 3.2.2 Data Processing Techniques **Qualitative Analysis**: The qualitative nature of the data necessitated careful categorization and interpretation. Verification success rates were broken down into individual task categories for detailed performance insights, helping identify patterns and trends specific to each condition. - **Quality (REF/size)**: This metric measured the relative improvement in quality over different conditions. It was found that both plain search and B-search with tools performed better than other methods, indicating a significant advantage in their ability to optimize network structure without exhaustive validation. - **False Acceptance Rate (FAR)**: This rate was crucial for assessing the reliability of results, especially in scenarios where slight deviations from expected outcomes could still be deemed acceptable. The study found that D+ world model conditions had notably lower FARs compared to other methods, suggesting a more robust and consistent approach to network validation. - **Depth Analysis**: By measuring the depth of comparisons made by each method, it was observed that C+ verified library networks consistently demonstrated higher depths, indicating greater computational efficiency. This finding underscored the importance of leveraging validated libraries for improving network performance. **Quantitative Metrics**: The quantitative metrics provided a clear picture of the overall performance trends across different conditions. - **Wall-clock Speed (WC)**: While not directly compared due to its immateriality in the study's context, it was noted that conditions with lower WC times generally corresponded to higher quality results. This finding highlighted the importance of computational efficiency as an underlying factor influencing network discovery. - **Memory Usage**: The memory usage varied significantly across conditions, particularly with the inclusion of world models and verified libraries. These methods demonstrated more efficient use of resources compared to plain searches, indicating that context-specific enhancements could lead to significant improvements in resource utilization.
