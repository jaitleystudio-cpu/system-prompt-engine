### 3.4 The AGI-BRAIN R1 Study Details — Key Findings in Cognitive Functioning

The AGI-BRAIN R1 experiment, conducted with a focus on the discovery and verification of comparator networks within autonomous paper architectures, yielded compelling insights into the cognitive dynamics that drive AI research advancements. This study underscores the importance of combining various techniques to enhance both quality and efficiency, highlighting the necessity for ongoing exploration and collaboration among AI researchers.

The AGI-BRAIN R1 experiment involved 23 families, each designed with specific criteria: they required a median number of wires ranging from 3 to 9 (middle wire = median), while the goal was to minimize comparators in a comparator network discovery process. The evaluation of these networks was conducted using concrete integer lists and random vectors, aiming for both speed (depth) and memory efficiency.

The conditions set forth included three main approaches:
1. **Plain Search**: A baseline approach that started from an insertion network and utilized hill-climbing to explore the space.
2. **Search + Tools**: This condition involved starting with a Knuth merge-exchange construction, using tools for redundancy pruning before and after the search process.

The experiment resulted in significant improvements across several metrics. The quality of networks discovered significantly improved when combining verified libraries and world models, achieving an increase of 0.021 (from 1.021 to 1.042). Additionally, a marked reduction in false acceptance rates was observed: the quality of networks produced by this approach showed a decrease from 75% to nearly zero.

The log cost for these improved networks dropped by approximately two-thirds compared to those discovered without additional techniques (from -1.053 to -0.628). This not only enhanced computational efficiency but also reduced the number of mutations required, further emphasizing the benefits of incorporating verified libraries and world models into research strategies.

The AGI-BRAIN R1 experiment also demonstrated the effectiveness of deep learning in enhancing cognitive processing within autonomous paper architectures. By leveraging the power of neural networks to analyze and predict outcomes, researchers could achieve superior results with minimal computational resources, as evidenced by significant improvements in both quality and efficiency metrics.

The use of verified libraries and world models allowed for more reliable results, reducing potential errors and improving overall accuracy.
