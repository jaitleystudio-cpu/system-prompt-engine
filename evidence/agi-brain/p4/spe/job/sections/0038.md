### Introduction to Comprehensivescope Techniques

Comprehensive scope techniques (CSC) are a set of methodologies designed to ensure that software systems can be verified, tested, and validated with high confidence. This section delves into the comprehensive coverage provided by CSC tools, including how they address potential failure modes, admissible probes, independent verification oracles, and more.

### Mechanism A: Reusable Counterexample Library

The **Reusable Counterexample Library** is a cornerstone of CSC. Whenever CSIC uncovers genuine blind spots or superficial compliance tricks, the failure mode is codified into an immutable Counterexample Record. This record includes:

- Requirement ID + Incomplete Evidence Profile
- The Counterfactual World \( w_{\text{bad}} \)
- The Distinguishing Experiment \( q^* \)
- Applicability boundaries for future code compilation

This ensures that the failure modes are preserved and can be replicated in future scenarios, enhancing the robustness of the system.

### Mechanism B: Assumption-Sensitive Verification (Anti-Stale Proofs)

**Assumption-Sensitive Verification** addresses issues related to proof traces. Following July 2026 research (*Looping Is Not Reliability*), proof traces that do not track environmental dependencies degrade software over repeated revisions. Each verified fact is cryptographically bound to:

- The SourceCommit
- ToolVersion
- EnvProfile
- PolicySnapshot

This ensures that any dependency drift automatically triggers requalification instead of blind replay, maintaining the integrity and security of the system.

### Mechanism C: Independently Qualified Verification Oracles

**Independent Verification oracles** are proposed as a means to ensure that CSC is strictly forbidden from declaring its own generated tests "correct" solely because the test fails. These oracles provide independent validation, ensuring that any conclusions drawn about the correctness of the system can be trusted.

### Admissibility Invariants for Probe \( q \)

The **Admissibility Invariants** for probe \( q \) are critical in determining its suitability:

- Obligation-Bound: Targets a real obligation derived from Protected Intent.
- Authority-Gated: Permitted by user privacy policy and hardware access rights.
- Cost-Bounded: Executable within the remaining `NanoUSD` budget.
- Independently Evaluable: Not judged by the same stochastic model that authored the code.

### Information-Theoretic Optimization

**Information-Theoretic Optimization** is used to find the best admissible probe \( q^* \) for a given distinguishing experiment:

\[ q^* = \arg\max_{q \in \mathcal{Q}_{\text{admissible}}} \frac{\widehat{\Delta V}(q) + \lambda \widehat{V}_{\text{reuse}}(q)}{C_{\text{total}}(q)} \]

Where:
- \( \widehat{\Delta V}(q) \): Decision-relevant uncertainty eliminated.
- \( \widehat{V}_{\text{reuse}}(q) \): Amortized value for future qualification runs.
- \( C_{\text{total}}(q) \): Total execution, verification, and risk cost.

### Verification of Admissible Probes

**Verification of Admissible Probes** is a critical aspect of the mechanism. This ensures that the probing strategies used are not only admissible but also effective in identifying failures without compromising the security or functionality of the system.

### Independent Oracle Qualification

The **Independent Oracle Qualifier** gate prevents any test from being declared "correct" by an independent oracle, ensuring that verification outcomes are trustworthy and reliable.

### Dual Search Mechanism

The dual search mechanism combines two competing searches to efficiently find admissible probes:

- WDIC Forward Synthesis: How can we establish the objective at minimal cost?
- CSC Adversarial Challenge: How could this apparent success still be wrong?

This approach ensures that both potential failure modes and blind spot exposure are thoroughly investigated, leading to more comprehensive coverage of the system's behavior.
