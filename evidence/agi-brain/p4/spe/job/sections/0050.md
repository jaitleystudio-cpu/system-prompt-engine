## 6.1 Core Library and Universal ABI Free Access Policy Explanation — Introduction to Core Library

### 6.1 Core Library and Universal ABI Free Access Policy #### Introduction to Core Library The **Verified Research in the System Prompt Engine (SPE)** project has a comprehensive approach to ensure that its core library and universal ABI remain free for all users, fostering a zero-cost ecosystem. This policy underscores the belief that accessibility should not come at the cost of innovation or quality. ### 1. Accessing the Core Library The **core library** comprises the fundamental building blocks necessary for SPE's operation, including utilities, models, and data structures. It is meticulously designed to be lightweight yet comprehensive, ensuring it can run locally without requiring any paid cloud services or expensive APIs. #### Free Access Policy The **FREE core** principle underpins this policy, which means that users do not have to pay a single penny for building and running SPE on their local machines. This includes all the necessary libraries and dependencies required for development. The goal is to empower developers by removing financial barriers, allowing them to experiment without worrying about costs. ### 2. Universal ABI The **Universal ABI (Application Binary Interface)** ensures that any version of the core library can seamlessly integrate with other parts of SPE, maintaining consistency across different environments. This approach is crucial for interoperability and ease of integration. #### Free Access Policy Just like the core library, the universal ABI remains free to use. This means developers do not have to pay additional fees or run their applications on cloud-based services that could incur significant costs. The policy ensures that every user can access the same set of tools without needing to upgrade or replace their existing infrastructure. ### 3. Validation and Conformance To ensure quality, SPE conducts rigorous validation and conformance checks using the `network_mode=NONE` configuration in Sprint proofs. This method guarantees that any proof developed within SPE adheres to the core library's specifications without requiring external dependencies like paid package registries or cloud-based services. #### Free Access Policy This policy ensures that all validation and conformance tests can be conducted locally, on a clean checkout. Developers do not need access to ad-targeting SDKs or other expensive tools, making it easier for them to create reproducible proofs without additional costs. ### 4. Dependencies While the core library and universal ABI are free, developers may occasionally require dependencies that cannot be provided in their current state due to licensing restrictions or local environment needs. The **free dep** strategy allows for these exceptions, ensuring that SPE remains accessible even when certain required libraries are not available locally. #### Free Access Policy The policy ensures that these cases can be managed gracefully through free open dependencies declared at the beginning of the project. This approach maintains the core library's and universal ABI's accessibility while allowing developers to work with additional tools as needed. ### 5. Sprint Proofs Sprint proofs, which are essential for demonstrating proof correctness and reliability, must remain reproducible offline on a clean checkout. This policy ensures that any errors or bugs identified during the verification process can be easily replicated and fixed without requiring access to external resources or cloud-based services. #### Free Access Policy This policy is designed to maintain the integrity of the verification process by allowing developers to recreate failed proofs locally, making it easier to identify and fix issues early on. This proactive approach reduces the likelihood of costly rework later in the development lifecycle. ### 6. Proofs and Verification Processes The **Proof posture** for SPE includes all aspects necessary to ensure that any proof developed within the project adheres to the core library's specifications. This includes documentation, conformance JSONL files, and reproducible local proofs. #### Free Access Policy This policy ensures that developers can create and maintain proofs that are both accurate and reliable without needing access to expensive or ad-targeting services. The ability to reproduce failed proofs locally is a critical feature of this policy, as it allows for early detection and correction of issues before they become major problems. ### 7. Verification Verdicts (VVD) The **Verification Verdicts (VVD)** framework provides detailed feedback on why certain models or proofs fail during the verification process.
