## 6.5 Core Library and Universal ABI Free Access Policy Explanation — Implementation Details: How the Policy Works

### 6.5 Core Library and Universal ABI Free Access Policy Explanation — Implementation Details: How the Policy Works The core library and universal ABI free access policy, known as LVT-3, is a cornerstone of System Prompt Engine (SPE) development and maintenance. This policy ensures that developers can create more effective applications without incurring unnecessary costs, fostering innovation, collaboration, and rapid adoption of technology across various industries. #### 1. **Zero-Cost Product Law Owner Target** The primary goal of the LVT-3 policy is to ensure a zero-cost product for the core SPE development and conformance efforts. This means that all core library components and universal ABI remain free to build and run locally without any paid cloud, API keys, analytics, or CI extras required. #### 2. **Free Core** The local build and runtime environment are entirely free of cost vectors such as dependency fees, hosting fees, or additional paid services. This includes the `pyproject.toml` file which must be declared as a free/open dependency, unless it's truly necessary for running SPE locally without violating any dependencies. #### 3. **No Paid Package Registry Deps** The policy explicitly prohibits the use of paid package registry dependencies within the core library or universal ABI. This ensures that all components are sourced through open repositories and can be easily updated without additional fees. #### 4. **Paid API Keys (LLM, Search, Billing)** Core software is free from any paid access to LLMs, search APIs, billing systems, or other paid services. This prevents the monetization of prompts or treating them as a SKU in the core. #### 5. **No Paid Hosting / SaaS Required** The policy also forbids the inclusion of hosting services or Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) providers for proofs. This ensures that all validation and conformance runs can be performed locally without additional fees. #### 6. **Dependencies Declaration** Core components must declare their dependencies in a free/open fashion, ensuring there are no hidden costs associated with local development. Additionally, the policy specifies that `jsonschema` should remain as part of the free dependency list unless it's truly required for running SPE on local machines. #### 7. **No Paid Prompts or Ad Targeting** Core software must not include any paid prompts or targeting advertising SDKs and pipelines. This ensures that all interactions are transparent and ethical, adhering to user privacy policy and hardware access rights. #### Implementation Details: ##### Local Python, Local Fixtures, Local Tempfile Adapters The policy requires that local Python implementations, local fixtures for testing, and local tempfile adapters already be in the tree of SPE core. This ensures a seamless integration and avoids any issues related to external dependencies or costs associated with local development. ##### Free Open Dependencies Already Declared All other components required by SPE core must have free/open dependencies declared upfront, ensuring there are no hidden fees or additional costs for developers who wish to contribute to the project or use its APIs without incurring extra expenses. #### 8. **Evidence-Compatible Worlds** The LVT-3 policy introduces a new concept of "evidence-compatible worlds" where SPE searches for pairs of worlds that align with protected human objectives while maintaining identical observed evidence. This approach ensures formal inconclusiveness when all visible tests pass, thereby providing a robust and transparent verification mechanism. #### 9. **Counterfactual Challenge Record (CCR)** The policy explicitly defines the Counterfactual Challenge Record (CCR) as an essential component in SPE's validation process. CCRs are used to track and record counterexamples that arise during proof attempts, ensuring that developers can learn from these instances without any additional costs. #### 10. **Witness-Directed Intelligence Compilation (WDIC)** The Witness-Directed Intelligence Compilation (WDIC) is a crucial feature in SPE's architecture, allowing for the synthesis of evidence-proving procedures. WDIC ensures that all execution, verification, and risk costs are minimized within the remaining budget, providing developers with a cost-effective solution without compromising their innovation. #### 11. **Counterfactual Specification Closure (CSC)** The Counterfactual Specification Closure (CSC) process is designed to ensure that any potential flaws in SPE can be identified early on during development and validation stages. This process involves synthesizing counterfactual worlds where the observed evidence remains unchanged, but the behavior differs from the protected human objective. #### 12.
