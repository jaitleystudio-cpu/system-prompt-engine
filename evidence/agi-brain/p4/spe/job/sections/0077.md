### 8.8 Cited Prior Art in the Field of Research Intelligence — Investigation into Reasoning Systems for Intelligent Decision Making The field of research intelligence has seen significant advancements over the years, driven by a multitude of innovative methodologies and techniques aimed at enhancing understanding and analysis of complex information structures. Among these, the system prompt engine (SPE) stands out as a notable contribution to the field, particularly in its ability to evaluate knowledge graphs through textual reasoning tasks. One key aspect of SPE's performance is its use of frontier reasoning, where multiple methods are employed simultaneously to enhance the overall effectiveness of model-driven decision-making processes. This approach is distinct from traditional methods such as search, constructions, and transfer, which contribute collectively towards achieving better outcomes in complex problem-solving scenarios (Figure 1). Frontier reasoning in SPE involves integrating various techniques that work together to tackle intricate challenges. For instance, the `agi_brain/netsearch.py` conditions A–D are utilized within SPE's architecture, ensuring a robust and adaptable system capable of handling diverse inputs effectively. Additionally, SPE utilizes research intelligence through the live scholarly lanes (`r*-scholarly` branches), which provide citation tables for easy reference. This feature is crucial in maintaining academic integrity while providing up-to-date information on relevant literature (Figure 2). Experimental intelligence in SPE encompasses a comprehensive understanding of knowledge graphs through continuous learning and adaptation, facilitated by the CSC world/discriminator (`research/csc/world_discriminator.py`) concept combined with LVT-3 stats (`research/lvt3/experiment.py: ci_t, ci_boot, _sd`). The model employs a condition D counterexample world model with discriminating-experiment ordering (CEGIS-style), enabling effective learning and robust decision-making under various scenarios. This approach significantly enhances the system's ability to handle high-consequence decisions by ensuring that even rare but critical errors are mitigated through continuous feedback mechanisms. Creative invention within SPE is exemplified by the LVT-3 Registry pattern, where train solutions adhere only after undergoing re-verification (Figure 3). The library in this case admits training solutions under the condition of independent evaluator acceptance. This stringent requirement underscores the importance of meticulous validation and verification processes that are essential for maintaining high standards of quality and reliability. Long-horizon autonomy is another critical feature provided by SPE, as evidenced by its use of the WDIC continuation engine (`research/wdic_vct/continuation_engine.py`). The resumable job (R7 slice) enabled through `agi_brain/longdoc.py` ensures that decisions can be revisited and refined over extended periods without losing context or information (Figure 4). Failure learning within SPE is further enhanced by the incorporation of a Failure Genome, which tracks independent rejections in the form of false_success. This mechanism allows for effective failure learning and safety through continuous improvement and adaptation based on real-world outcomes (Figure 5). The integration of this system underscores the importance of both theoretical insights and practical applications in advancing research intelligence. In summary, the SPE system is a significant contribution to the field of research intelligence, particularly in its ability to evaluate knowledge graphs through textual reasoning tasks. Its innovative approaches such as frontier reasoning, experimental intelligence, creative invention, long-horizon autonomy, and failure learning make it an invaluable tool for researchers looking to extract meaningful insights from complex information structures.
