## 9.1 Statistics Used in Validation and Evaluation Methods — Introduction to Statistical Analysis Techniques

### 9.1 Statistics Used in Validation and Evaluation Methods: Introduction to Statistical Analysis Techniques In this handbook, we delve into various statistical techniques that are used extensively for validating and evaluating research results generated through systems prompts engines (SPE). These tools help ensure the reliability and accuracy of outcomes by providing quantitative measures of performance metrics such as cost, quality, and other relevant indicators. #### 9.1.1 Mean Over Test Families Per Seed The **mean over test families per seed** is a crucial metric used to evaluate the overall performance of different seeds in terms of their average quality scores. This statistical measure helps identify which seeds generally yield higher-quality results across multiple tests conducted under varying conditions. - **Quality (REF/size)**: If independently validated, this reflects the pruned merge-exchange size or frozen table content from a test family. - **False Success (share of claimed networks)**: The share of network claims that are ultimately rejected by the independent evaluator is an important metric. It helps in identifying seeds with high false acceptance rates, indicating potential areas for improvement. #### 9.1.2 Cost Cost refers to the total metered input evaluations performed during the evaluation process. This includes not only the computational resources but also metrics like time and memory usage. Understanding cost effectively is crucial as it directly impacts both the execution time and the energy consumption of the system, thus contributing to environmental impact assessments. - **Total Metered Input Evaluations (generator side, including library construction is NOT counted for C/D)**: This metric helps in understanding the efficiency of different evaluation methods. - **Log Cost per Seed**: This provides a logarithmic breakdown of cost per seed, which can be used to identify seeds that contribute disproportionately to overall costs. #### 9.1.3 Paired Differences and Confidence Intervals The paired differences over 10 seeds are analyzed using **t-CI (df=9)** for statistical significance testing. This method helps in determining whether the differences observed between different seeds are statistically significant, providing a robust basis for decision-making based on these results. - **Paired Bootstrap 95% CI**: The bootstrap confidence interval, with 10,000 resamples and random number seed 12345, offers an estimate of uncertainty around the mean differences. This method is particularly useful when dealing with small sample sizes or non-normal distributions. - **Bonferroni Adjusted t-CI**: To correct for multiple comparisons, this adjusted confidence interval ensures that any observed effects are not due to random chance. #### 9.1.4 Hypotheses and Decision Rules The decision rules based on the Bonferroni CI help in making informed decisions regarding whether a particular hypothesis is supported within the tested scope or if it needs further investigation. It indicates areas where performance improvements could be achieved without further research or development. #### 9.1.6 Ground Truth and Independence The ground truth refers to the original reference implementation used by both the generator and evaluator modules, ensuring that there are no discrepancies in outputs due to differences between these implementations. - **Generator Module Samples (family, params) and Visible Inputs**: The generator module samples inputs based on family parameters and visible inputs. - **Evaluator Module Owns Every Primitive Implementation**: This ensures that the evaluator can verify its own reference implementation of every primitive without relying on a shared interpreter or additional resources. #### 9.1.7 Configurations (Matched Budgets) Configurations are evaluated under matched budgets, with each config receiving exactly k=4 oracle probes and facing common loops for candidate consistency finding and probe application.
