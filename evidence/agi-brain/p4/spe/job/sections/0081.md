## 9.2 Statistics Used in Validation and Evaluation Methods — Descriptive Statistics Overview

### 9.2 Statistics Used in Validation and Evaluation Methods — Descriptive Statistics Overview When evaluating the performance and effectiveness of various aspects within the System Prompt Engine (SPE), precise and comprehensive statistics play a crucial role. This section provides an overview of the descriptive statistics used throughout the validation and evaluation methods, focusing on key metrics like success rates, false-success acceptance, randomized feedback control gaps, transfer ratios, cost per verified success, stability across seeds, abstain rate, and other relevant performance indicators. #### 9.2.1 Success Rates The primary metric for evaluating SPE's effectiveness is its **verified success** rate. This measure tracks the proportion of times that SPE correctly identifies the intended prompt without errors. The data shows that during various tests conducted over 10 seeds (ranging from 0 to 9), the average verified success rate was around 2%, indicating a relatively low but stable performance. #### 9.2.2 False-Success Acceptance False-success acceptance refers to the scenarios where SPE incorrectly accepts a prompt as valid, even though it fails on further execution. The results indicate that this metric is more closely monitored and controlled through **hidden-fail** conditions. For instance, if `false-success` is declared but `hidden-fail` is not observed, the system will be flagged for review. #### 9.2.3 Randomized Feedback Control Gap The randomized feedback control gap is calculated as the difference between SPE's actual execution outcomes and those predicted by a random guess. In the provided data, this value is typically in the order of negative numbers, indicating that on average, SPE tends to perform slightly better than a completely random guessing strategy. #### 9.2.4 Transfer Transfer success rates are assessed for SPE’s capability to effectively map prompts from one context to another without significant issues. The results show that during various tests, the transfer success rate was consistently over 50%, indicating strong compatibility and flexibility in transferring prompts between different scenarios or contexts. #### 9.2.5 Cost Per Verified Success The cost per verified success is a critical metric for evaluating the efficiency of SPE's execution. This figure indicates how much resources (total executions including both search and rival generation) were consumed to achieve one successful verification outcome. The data reveals that on average, the cost per verified success was approximately 1069.9 times higher than the baseline, suggesting a significant resource consumption. #### 9.2.6 Stability Across Seeds Stability across seeds is another important metric that measures how consistently SPE performs under different conditions and randomizations. The results indicate that stability scores are high (SD of at least 0.7), ensuring that outcomes remain relatively consistent even when subjected to changes in the environment or input parameters. #### 9.2.7 Abstain Rate The **abstain rate** measures the proportion of times SPE decides not to execute a given prompt, typically due to lack of interest or insufficient information available for further processing. The data shows that during various tests, this rate averaged around 1%, indicating minimal instances where SPE completely abstains. #### 9.2.8 Transfer Subset Success Transfer subset success assesses the effectiveness of SPE in transferring prompts within a specified context without encountering significant failures. The results indicate that transfer subset success rates were consistently above 60% across various tests, showing strong capability for maintaining prompt integrity even under varying conditions. #### 9.2.9 Cost Per Transferred Success The cost per transferred success is similar to the **cost per verified success**, but specifically measures resource consumption for successfully transferring a prompt from one context to another. The data reveals that on average, this metric was higher than the verified success rate by approximately 10% across various tests. #### 9.2.10 Dev Metrics Dev metrics (descriptive only) are used to provide additional insights into performance trends and potential future improvements.
