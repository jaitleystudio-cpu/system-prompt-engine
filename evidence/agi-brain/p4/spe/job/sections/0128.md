## 13.9 Conditions for Hard Rules Compliance: Free Core, No Paid Expenditures — Limitations of Current Research Methodologies

### 13.9 Conditions for Hard Rules Compliance: Free Core, No Paid Expenditures — Limitations of Current Research Methodologies In our comprehensive research into the System Prompt Engine (SPE), we aim to maintain transparency and ethical integrity in all aspects of development and implementation. This includes adhering strictly to a set of hard rules designed to ensure that core SPE components remain free from paid dependencies or expenditures. #### 13.9.1 Free Core Library The first rule stipulates that the **SPE core library** must be entirely free for users to build, run, and deploy locally without any cost or need for additional licenses or APIs. This applies not just to running SPE itself but also to all components within it—such as third-party libraries like `jsonschema` and `pytest`. The only requirement is that the local dependencies are declared openly in the project's `pyproject.toml`. #### 13.9.2 Free Open Dependencies The second rule ensures that any open-source or free dependencies required for development and testing must be clearly documented and available for all users to adopt without additional fees. This includes external libraries and tools that contribute to the SPE ecosystem, such as `jsonschema` and `pytest`. The critical point is that these dependencies should not be restricted or excluded because they are necessary. #### 13.9.3 No Paid Clouds The third rule forbids the inclusion of any paid cloud services in core SPE components. This includes tools for running proofs, APIs, analytics, CI extras, and advertising SDKs. The goal here is to ensure that users can develop their applications without relying on expensive or proprietary cloud resources. #### 13.9.4 No Paid LLMs / Model APIs The fourth rule prevents the use of paid language models (LLMs) or model APIs within core SPE components. This includes not only direct calls but also indirect dependencies that pass through these services for processing. The rationale is to keep SPE focused on its primary purpose: providing a robust, free, and ethical AI system. #### 13.9.5 No Paid Analytics The fifth rule prohibits the integration of any paid analytics tools or services into core SPE components. This includes not only direct use but also indirect dependencies that pass through these services for analysis or processing. The focus is on maintaining an unbiased and free approach to data collection and analysis. #### 13.9.6 No Paid CI Extras The sixth rule strictly forbids the inclusion of paid Continuous Integration (CI) extras in core SPE components. This includes not only direct dependencies but also indirect ones that pass through these services for code quality checks, automated testing, or other development tools. #### 13.9.7 Ad Targeting Restrictions The seventh and final restriction ensures that core SPE does **not** include any advertising SDKs or user-targeting ad pipelines. This is crucial to maintaining a completely ethical approach to data collection and analysis, ensuring that all interactions with users are transparent and without bias. #### Limitations of Current Research Methodologies Despite these stringent rules, there are inherent limitations in current research methodologies. One significant limitation is the reliance on external verification tools such as `WDIC` (Witness-Directed Intelligence Compilation) and `CSC` (Counterfactual Specification Closure). While these methods aim to enhance the self-improving intelligence of SPE, they inherently require a certain level of knowledge about how users interact with the system. This can lead to situations where the verification process is not fully free from paid dependencies or expenditures. Another limitation arises from the nature of counterfactual testing itself. When an experiment's outcome differs significantly (as measured by **False Acceptance Rate** [FAR] and **True Acceptance Rate** [TAR]), it may be necessary to conduct additional experiments in a new world (counterfactual world) that closely matches the original but with modified inputs or outputs. This process can introduce dependencies on paid resources, as even the verification of a single experiment might require access to certain tools or services. Furthermore, while the current research is focused on developing **Witness-Directed Intelligence Compilation** and ensuring compliance with these hard rules, the limitations in our methodologies are still present. These include the need for external verification tools like `WDIC` and `CSC`, which necessitate dependencies that could potentially breach our free core rule.
