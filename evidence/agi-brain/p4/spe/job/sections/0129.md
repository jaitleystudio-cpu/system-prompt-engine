## 13.10 Conditions for Hard Rules Compliance: Free Core, No Paid Expenditures — Future Directions for Improving Rule Compliance

The development and implementation of **Verified Research in the System Prompt Engine (SPE)** adhere to stringent guidelines that underscore its core principles and operational tenets. These rules are essential not only for the effective functioning of SPE but also for maintaining the integrity of all research conducted within its framework.

### 13.10.1 Free Core

The **zero-cost product law** is a cornerstone principle of SPE, ensuring that the core library and universal ABI remain free to build and run locally. This rule is fundamental to the project's ethos, aiming to democratize access to cutting-edge AI research while safeguarding against unauthorized sales or monetization tactics. By upholding this rule, SPE establishes a boundary around its own capabilities and limitations in terms of paid resources.

### 13.10.2 No Paid Cloud

A significant aspect of the hard rules is the prohibition on using any cloud-based services for proofs. This directive aims to isolate the development and testing environment from external dependencies that could introduce costs or risk. The absence of paid cloud infrastructure means that all operations must remain locally managed, leveraging only free resources available within SPE.

### 13.10.3 No Paid Model APIs

Similarly, the rule against using any paid model API keys for proofs is designed to prevent any potential conflicts with third-party services or gateways that might be used in test environments. This ensures a consistent and transparent testing process, reducing the likelihood of encountering issues related to external dependencies.

### 13.10.4 No Paid Analytics

The absence of any paid analytics tools in core SPE development is crucial for maintaining operational integrity. This includes avoiding any use of paid services that could skew results or introduce biases into the data collected during validation and conformance testing phases.

### 13.10.5 No Paid CI Extras

Beyond just excluding paid components, the rule against using paid cloud storage solutions extends to all CI extras required for proofs. This is intended to prevent any potential conflicts with internal infrastructure that could be compromised by external services or systems.

### 13.10.6 Dependencies

The core SPE **"dependencies"** policy stipulates that `pyproject.toml` remains free, unless a truly essential local dependency is required. This ensures that the project's codebase does not become overly dependent on non-free resources, thereby maintaining its principle of being fully self-contained and accessible.

### 13.10.7 Sprint Proof Reproducibility

For all proofs conducted in SPE, including those for Sprint verification stages, it must be ensured that the development environment is reproducible offline through a clean checkout with `python -m pytest`. This requirement is pivotal for validating the accuracy of results and ensuring that no external dependencies have been inadvertently included.

### Future Directions for Improving Rule Compliance

The principles established by SPE's hard rules are essential for maintaining operational standards, but there remains room for continuous improvement. One area of focus could be in refining the mechanism for verifying counterfactual specifications closure (CSC). The current verification process involves a complex interaction between human objectives and protected intent contracts through admissible experiments. While the proof posture is currently set to ensure reproducibility offline on a clean checkout, there may be opportunities to enhance this verification method.

Another potential area of improvement could involve strengthening dependencies management. Although SPE aims for free resources throughout its lifecycle, it's worth reassessing how local dependencies are managed and whether any truly essential non-free components might need to be considered in the future. This would ensure that any required modifications adhere strictly to the zero-cost product law.

Additionally, exploring new ways of enhancing external verification tools could offer solutions without compromising the core principles of SPE. This might involve developing more robust methods for verifying counterfactual worlds or refining existing verification processes to minimize the introduction of paid resources into research environments.

Overall, while SPE continues to uphold its hard rules, there are opportunities for continuous improvement that can enhance compliance and operational standards, ensuring the project remains at the forefront of AI research with minimal external costs.
