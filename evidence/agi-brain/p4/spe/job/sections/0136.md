### 14.7 Evidence and Results from Raw Data Analysis — Evaluation of Raw Data Correlation with Reported Results

#### Introduction

The evaluation of raw data correlation is a critical step in validating hypotheses derived from machine learning models, such as those used within the SPE system. This section delves into how we assess the consistency between reported results and findings based on the analyzed raw data.

#### Evidence Analysis

The primary evidence for evaluating the raw data analysis comes from several sources, including:

1. **Run Logs**: Detailed logs of every run provide critical insights into the performance metrics such as cost/verified (the ratio of verified tasks to total tasks), false-success rate, and other key indicators that directly impact the overall success of a task.
   
2. **Output Files**: Raw output files from the runs capture significant detail on what was executed and observed during each run. These include log entries for stdout, stderr, and any diagnostic information, which can indicate issues or successes.

3. **Comparison with Reported Results**: The comparison method involves analyzing the reported results against the actual raw data obtained from the runs. This includes cross-referencing the means and standard deviations of various metrics to ensure that the reported outcomes are consistent with the observed performance in the raw data logs.

#### Evaluation Process

The evaluation process is conducted through a series of steps:

1. **Data Extraction**: The raw data files generated during each run are extracted for analysis, focusing on specific metrics related to task success and cost efficiency.
   
2. **Metric Calculation**: For each metric (e.g., verified tasks, false-success rate), the calculated values from the raw logs are compared with those reported in the original results.

3. **Statistical Analysis**:
   - **T-Tests (paired):** These tests ensure that the differences between reported and observed outcomes are statistically significant.
   - **Bootstrapping:** This method provides a more robust measure of confidence by resampling data, ensuring that the observed correlation is not due to random chance.

4. **Reported Outcomes vs. Raw Data**: The comparison reveals discrepancies or non-correspondences. For instance:
   - If the reported results indicate 98% success but the raw data shows a significantly lower percentage (e.g., 90%), this discrepancy may suggest an error in reporting.
   - Conversely, if the raw data indicates much higher performance than claimed, it could be due to optimization or additional context that was not included.

#### Examples

- **Example 1**: Consider System E and System B. If System E reports a 2135.6 cost/verified ratio but the actual observed result shows significantly lower costs (e.g., 891.5), it indicates overoptimization or misuse of resources.
  
- **Example 2**: For false-success rates, if reported as 0.0133 but shown to be much higher in the raw data (e.g., 0.80 for E_shuf) suggests that System E abstains on a large number of tasks not included in the reported results.

#### Conclusion

The evaluation of raw data correlation is crucial in validating hypotheses and ensuring the reliability of machine learning outcomes. Through thorough analysis, we can identify discrepancies between reported results and observed performance, allowing us to refine models or methodologies where necessary. This method also strengthens trust in the scientific validity of our findings by providing concrete evidence that supports or refutes claims made during the research process.
