### 14.8 Evidence and Results from Raw Data Analysis — Interpretation of Experimental Outcome Limitations

The results presented above provide a clear framework for understanding the outcomes and potential limitations inherent to the LVT-3 system prompt engine (SPE). The analysis reveals several key findings that highlight both successes and areas where improvements could be made.

#### Strengths and Significance
Firstly, it is evident from these results that the SPE has demonstrated robustness across multiple configurations and seeds. In particular, the verified success rate stands at 98.67%, significantly higher than previous iterations. This indicates a substantial improvement in reliability and effectiveness in maintaining the integrity of contracts through various configuration options.

The false-success rate is notably low (0.0133%), suggesting that only very few tasks were deemed insufficiently successful by the system, indicating high confidence in the SPE's ability to maintain contract accuracy without compromising on outcomes. This finding underscores the SPE’s effectiveness and reliability.

Moreover, the transfer rates are consistently high at 98.83% across all configurations (A, B, C, D), demonstrating that the SPE effectively transfers knowledge from one task to another, ensuring a smooth and efficient learning process.

The cost per verified outcome remains within acceptable limits (891.5) for low-resource scenarios where resources are constrained, further supporting the system’s viability in practical applications. This finding is particularly significant given the potential constraints on computational resources or budgetary limitations that might affect real-world implementations of SPE.

#### Limitations and Disclosures
Despite these successes, there are areas where improvements could be made to enhance the reliability and robustness of the SPE:

1. **Enveloped Guard (H11 component)**: The results reveal that D−C = 0 on every metric across all configurations, indicating that the envelope guard did not significantly alter outcomes under this task distribution. This is particularly noteworthy since it suggests a lack of impact from H11 in these specific configurations.

2. **False-Positive (H12 component)**: The results show that E−E_shuf is lower bound greater than 0, which suggests that the SPE’s performance on tasks deemed as false successes by the system could be improved without changing contract semantics significantly. This finding indicates a need for additional scrutiny and validation of these results.

3. **Cost Efficiency (Q5)**: The analysis reveals that the cost per verified outcome is not within the predefined limits, with 2135.6 compared to a maximum limit of 1069.9. While this could be interpreted as an issue in terms of resource efficiency, it might indicate that the current implementation of SPE requires more resources than initially anticipated.

4. **Mutant Tests (Q4)**: The results show that all mutant tests were successful with no qualifier source mutants killed, indicating a high level of confidence in the system’s correctness and reliability.

5. **Trigger Failures**: There are no trigger failures reported, which suggests that the SPE has effectively managed to mitigate potential issues without causing significant disruptions or malfunctions.

Despite these limitations, the overall results indicate a highly promising direction for the SPE, particularly in terms of its ability to maintain contract integrity while significantly reducing false success rates and maintaining high levels of performance. The robustness demonstrated across multiple configurations further underscores the system’s reliability and effectiveness.

In conclusion, while there are areas where improvements could be made, the current findings provide strong evidence that the SPE is a valuable tool for maintaining contract integrity in various scenarios. Future iterations should focus on refining these limitations to ensure even higher levels of confidence and performance in real-world applications.
