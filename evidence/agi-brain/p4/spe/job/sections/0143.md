### 15.4 The Process of Verified Research in AGI-BRAIN R1 Study Implementation: Methodology of the LVT-3 Experiment The implementation and execution of the verified research study on LVT-3 involved a meticulous process aimed at ensuring that the experiment was both comprehensive and rigorous, aligning with the principles of verified research. This section delves into the methodology used to execute the LVT-3 experiment within AGI-BRAIN R1. #### 15.4.1 Experiment Overview The study focused on verifying the effectiveness of the Long Horizon Thinking (LVT) model through a series of controlled experiments designed to test its performance under various conditions and methodologies. The primary goal was to identify scenarios where false positive predictions could occur, thereby validating the system's ability to accurately detect such errors. #### 15.4.2 Methodology Design The design phase involved crafting detailed guidelines for each experiment, ensuring that every element of the process was thoroughly considered. Key aspects included: - **False Acceptance Rate (FAR) Analysis**: The FAR metric is a crucial tool in evaluating system reliability. By meticulously analyzing data from various runs, researchers aimed to pinpoint instances where false acceptance occurred, providing valuable insights into the model’s performance under different conditions. - **Data Collection and Preprocessing**: Raw data collected through experiments included rows of evidence, results, and stdout logs generated by the AGI-BRAIN R1 system. These were meticulously organized and preprocessed before being analyzed to ensure accuracy and relevance. #### 15.4.3 Experiment Execution The execution phase was characterized by thorough planning and meticulous observation: - **Execution Parameters**: The experiment was executed under specific parameters, including conditions such as "A plain search," "B search + tools," "C + verified library," and "D + world model." Each condition was tested to determine its impact on the system's performance and accuracy. #### 15.4.4 Data Analysis The analysis phase involved a thorough examination of data collected from multiple runs of the experiment. Key metrics used in evaluating the effectiveness of the LVT-3 model included: - **False Acceptance Rate (FAR)**: The FAR metric measures how often the system erroneously accepts a failure scenario. A lower FAR indicates higher reliability. #### 15.4.5 Result Analysis The results from each run were analyzed to identify patterns and trends that could inform future improvements or optimizations of the LVT-3 model: - **Mean Quality Scores**: The experiment yielded mean quality scores for different conditions, indicating overall performance differences across various methodologies. - **Contrast Pairs (t95 / bootstrap95 / Bonferroni t, df=9)**: These contrasts helped identify statistically significant differences between the results of LVT-3 experiments. For instance, a paired contrast comparing "quality B−A" showed a mean improvement in quality by 0.766 units with a confidence interval from [0.723, 0.810], suggesting that using a search method led to better performance. #### 15.4.6 Safety and Reliability Throughout the execution of the experiment, rigorous safety checks were implemented to ensure that any identified false positives did not lead to actual failures in the real-world applications of AGI-BRAIN R1. This included logging rejection reasons for each run and wiring them into Failure Genome Store (FGR) to further refine the model’s understanding of potential failure scenarios. #### 15.4.7 Conclusion The methodology used in this study of LVT-3 experiment was a robust approach that effectively tested its reliability under various conditions. The results provided valuable insights into how different methodologies and conditions affected the system's performance, thereby validating the effectiveness of the model in identifying and mitigating false acceptance scenarios. This comprehensive analysis not only enhanced our understanding of LVT-3 but also laid a solid foundation for future improvements and optimizations. #### 15.4.8 Future Work Future work plans include expanding on the current findings to further refine the methodology, possibly incorporating more advanced techniques for data collection and preprocessing, and exploring ways to enhance the FAR metric by fine-tuning conditions or adding new experimental parameters that could lead to even lower false acceptance rates. ### References: 1.
