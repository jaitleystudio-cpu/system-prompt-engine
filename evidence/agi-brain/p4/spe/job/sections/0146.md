### 15.7 The Process of Verified Research in AGI-BRAIN R1 Study Implementation — Verification Process in AGI-BRAIN R1 Study Implementation The AGI-BRAIN R1 study implemented a rigorous verification process to ensure that the experimental results were reliable and reproducible. This was achieved through several key steps, each designed to identify and rectify potential issues in the system’s performance. #### 15.7.1 Initial Experiment Design Before any research began, the team meticulously crafted an experiment design based on the AGI-BRAIN R1 contract. The study focused on three main aspects: discovering a valid comparator network with as few comparators as possible and maintaining depth within the constraints of parallel time (D), the size of the networks in terms of comparators (C), and the speed of execution (S). Each condition was evaluated using a combination of different validation methods, including exhaustive validity tools and redundancy-pruning before or after the search process. #### 15.7.2 Experiment Execution The experiment was executed on multiple seeds to ensure the robustness of the results. Each seed involved running the search algorithm with varying conditions (e.g., number of comparators, depth), which were varied across families ranging from 4 to 12 wires and median sizes in various combinations. The execution time was recorded for each run, allowing researchers to analyze performance trends. #### 15.7.3 Validation Methods To ensure the validity of the results, multiple validation methods were employed: - **Validation by Insertion Networks**: The initial search started from an insertion network. - **Exhaustive Validity Tool**: This tool checked the validity of the claims generated by the algorithm on a fixed 32-vector random sample. - **Redundancy-Pruning Tools**: These tools reduced redundancy in the candidate networks to ensure that only optimal solutions were evaluated. #### 15.7.4 Data Analysis The raw data from each run was analyzed for quality, false success rates, input evaluations, and depth. The results were then paired with contrasts to identify significant differences, such as changes in performance metrics like log cost or improved execution times due to the implementation of specific tools. #### 15.7.5 Statistical Analysis A detailed statistical analysis was conducted on the data collected from each run. This included measures for false acceptance rates (FAR), which indicated the proportion of experiments that were deemed successful even when they failed, leading to the conclusion that certain conditions or algorithms performed better than expected. #### 15.7.6 Failure Identification Identifying and rectifying potential failures was a crucial part of the process. This involved detecting omissions in requirements and invalidating closed proofs where necessary. For instance, if an experiment reported it as complete but lacked critical evidence, researchers would invalidate that proof to prevent false claims. #### 15.7.7 Data Visualization To better understand the relationships between different variables and outcomes, data was visualized using graphs and charts. This included heat maps showing performance trends across conditions, line plots tracking improvements in execution time with increased depth, and scatter plots illustrating the effect of varying network sizes on quality metrics. #### 15.7.8 Continuous Refinement The process did not stop after initial results were verified; continuous refinement was necessary to refine future research designs. This involved revisiting experiments that showed suboptimal performance, analyzing why certain conditions led to better outcomes, and adjusting parameters or methods based on new insights gained. #### 15.7.9 Final Report The final report synthesized the findings from all validation steps into a comprehensive document detailing the strengths and weaknesses of the AGI-BRAIN R1 system under different conditions. This report was peer-reviewed and used to inform further research design, ensuring that the empirical results provided actionable insights for improving future experiments. #### 15.7.10 Importance of Detailed Planning The successful implementation of these verification steps underscores the importance of meticulous planning in complex experimental studies. The methodology highlighted here not only identified strengths and weaknesses but also provided valuable insights for refining future research designs, ultimately ensuring that empirical findings were accurate and actionable. In summary, the AGI-BRAIN R1 study’s verified research process was comprehensive, involving multiple validation methods, statistical analysis, failure identification, data visualization, continuous refinement, and a detailed final report.
