# Visual review — round 3

This round used the scripted browser harness rather than initial-load screenshots. It entered a real request, invoked the Worker/WASM path, waited for the workspace, inspected Intent Lens, returned to the story, and captured Acts II, IV, VI–X.

## Resolved in this round

1. **Resolved — portrait scene crop.** The 390×844 composition now frames the raw workpiece and all six colored/shape-differentiated filaments behind the headline and intake plate.
2. **Resolved — Prompt Artifact face.** The compiled artifact is ceramic white with legible dark prompt text. Titanium/graphite layers sit behind it as shadows rather than painting over its face.
3. **Resolved — 320×568 collision.** The short-viewport composition intentionally removes supporting lede/runtime rows and keeps identity, headline, 3D filaments, raw-thought field, and compile control within the viewport.
4. **Resolved — real state evidence.** Workspace and Intent Lens captures show a valid Worker/WASM result and user-entered request rather than a decorative mock.
5. **Pass — fallback parity.** Reduced-motion and forced no-WebGL LITE both show an authored still composition with workpiece, gates, filaments, and ceramic output.
6. **Pass — matrix coverage.** Fresh captures cover 1440×900, 1920×1080, 2560×1440, tablet portrait/landscape, 390×844, 360×800, and 320×568.
7. **Pass — runtime cleanliness.** The browser harness recorded zero console errors and zero page errors.

## Remaining non-blocking defects

1. At 320×568, supporting explanatory copy and the runtime ledger are omitted to preserve the primary task and minimum touch geometry. They remain present at 360×800 and above.
2. Semantic filament crossings are intentionally dense on portrait screens; the text retains sufficient dark backing, but the composition is visually busier than the desktop cut.
3. Tablet quality depends on actual pointer/memory signals. The headless 768px viewport reported CINEMATIC because it emulates a fine pointer; a real coarse-pointer tablet resolves to STANDARD.
4. The procedural world does not use external texture maps or authored GLTF detail. This preserves the zero-asset budget but limits micro-surface richness compared with offline film rendering.

These are polish constraints, not functional or accessibility blockers. No 10/10 claim is made.
